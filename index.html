Professional Interview: Guy Bisuku — KBTrainings

Phone interview • Cybersecurity, networking, Cisco, Fortinet, and VMware

I interviewed Guy Bisuku from KBTrainings by phone to learn more about cybersecurity and networking careers. The interview helped me understand which technical and professional skills are important in real IT work.

What I Learned

One of the most important things I learned is that cybersecurity depends heavily on networking. Many attacks move through networks, so understanding how devices communicate can help a cybersecurity professional recognize security problems and troubleshoot them.

We also discussed Cisco, Fortinet, and VMware. Cisco is important for networking, Fortinet is used for firewalls and network security, and VMware is useful for virtualization. He explained that experience with these technologies can help someone become stronger in both IT and cybersecurity.

Certifications and Hands-On Practice

He recommended starting with certifications such as Cisco CCNA and Fortinet certifications. He also explained that certifications should be combined with hands-on practice. Building labs, troubleshooting systems, and working directly with the technology are important because employers need people who can apply what they know.

Skills That Matter

  • Networking
  • Troubleshooting
  • Problem-solving
  • Communication
  • Patience when investigating technical problems

AI and Cybersecurity

We also talked about AI. AI is becoming more useful for threat detection and data analysis, but cybersecurity professionals still need a strong understanding of networks, systems, and security fundamentals. AI should support the work, not replace the professional's knowledge.

My Reflection

The biggest surprise for me was learning how important networking is as a foundation for cybersecurity. Before the interview, I thought cybersecurity was mostly about security tools. After the interview, I understood that networking knowledge is essential. Because of this, I want to improve my networking skills, practice more labs, and continue learning about Cisco, Fortinet, VMware, and cybersecurity certifications.

Technology Paper

Are Passkeys Really Better Than Passwords?

Current draft • Microsoft Entra ID and passkeys

Research focus: I am investigating whether Microsoft Entra ID's move toward passkeys can make enterprise authentication more secure and practical than passwords and SMS-based multifactor authentication.

Introduction

Passwords are still one of the most common ways people sign in to online services, but they also create security problems. People can reuse passwords, forget them, or give them to attackers through phishing. SMS-based authentication can add protection, but codes can still be stolen or relayed. My investigation looks at whether passkeys provide a stronger and more practical approach for organizations using Microsoft Entra ID.

How Passkeys Work

Passkeys use public-key cryptography instead of depending on a password that a user has to remember and type. A public key is stored with the service, while the private key stays protected on the user's device or passkey provider. During sign-in, the private key proves the user's identity without being sent to the website.

This is important because there is no reusable password for a phishing website to collect. FIDO2 and WebAuthn are designed so authentication is connected to the legitimate service or domain, which makes common phishing attacks much harder.

Security Benefits

The strongest benefit I found is phishing resistance. Passwords and manually entered one-time codes can be stolen and reused. Passkeys work differently because the authentication process uses cryptographic keys tied to the real service. This reduces the usefulness of fake login pages that try to capture credentials.

My research also changed how I think about multifactor authentication. Before this investigation, I thought passkeys were simply another type of MFA. After comparing the technologies, I understood that the main difference is that a passkey does not ask the user to send a reusable secret or manually enter a code that an attacker could intercept.

Challenges and Trade-Offs

Passkeys are not a perfect solution. Organizations still need to think about device compatibility, older systems, lost devices, account recovery, privacy, employee training, and implementation costs. A company cannot assume that every employee will have the same device or the same passkey experience.

There is also a difference between synced and device-bound passkeys. Synced passkeys can be easier for users because they may work across devices. Device-bound credentials or hardware security keys may be more appropriate for some high-privilege accounts. This shows that the best solution can depend on the user's role and the organization's security needs.

Implementation

The technology may be strong, but a successful rollout still depends on good planning. Organizations should check device readiness, identify which users are ready, prepare recovery and backup authentication methods, test important applications, and train employees before enforcing a new authentication method across the organization.

Working Conclusion

Based on my investigation so far, passkeys appear to provide stronger protection against phishing and credential theft than traditional passwords and SMS-based authentication. However, stronger authentication does not remove the need for good planning. Organizations still need reliable recovery procedures, compatible devices, employee training, and policies that match the risk level of different users.
